Bedding, pointing and ridge caps

Ridge caps cover the meeting line of tiled roof planes. Bedding is the material that supports and positions the caps; pointing is the protective finishing material visible around them. These terms are often used together, but they are not interchangeable. A visible crack in pointing does not by itself prove that every ridge cap needs to be removed or rebedded.

Ground-level signs worth recording

Look for cracked or missing mortar, a cap that appears lifted or out of line, debris collecting near the ridge, fresh staining after rain or multiple defects along the same run. Photograph from ground level. Roof geometry, age, weather exposure and earlier repairs can all affect the condition of a ridge line.

Why a clear scope matters

A useful repair explanation identifies the affected ridge area, distinguishes observed conditions from assumptions, and explains what work is proposed. This avoids treating every crack as the same problem. It also helps homeowners compare written scopes rather than comparing slogans or broad claims.

Safe access is not optional

Ridge work is roof work at height. Roof tasks involve falls, access, weather, electricity and falling-object hazards, so Ellis plans access and site safety before work begins. Do not climb onto a tiled roof to press a ridge cap or scrape mortar. If a cap looks displaced after a storm, remain clear and contact Ellis Services Group.
