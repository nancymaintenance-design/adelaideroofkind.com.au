Ridge Capping: When to Repoint or Re-bed

What do cracked pointing and loose ridge caps mean for a tiled roof?

Short answer: cracked pointing may need repointing when the bedding and caps remain sound; loose caps or failed bedding need the support assessed first. Ellis checks both layers and nearby roof details before confirming the repair. Do not climb onto the roof to test a cap or scrape mortar.

Reviewed by Ellis Services Group

Bedding, pointing and ridge caps

Ridge caps cover the meeting line of tiled roof planes. Bedding is the material that supports and positions the caps; pointing is the protective finishing material visible around them. These terms are often used together, but they are not interchangeable. A visible crack in pointing does not by itself prove that every ridge cap needs to be removed or rebedded.

Ground-level signs worth recording

Look for cracked or missing mortar, a cap that appears lifted or out of line, debris collecting near the ridge, fresh staining after rain or multiple defects along the same run. Photograph from ground level. Roof geometry, age, weather exposure and earlier repairs can all affect the condition of a ridge line.

Why a clear scope matters

A useful repair explanation identifies the affected ridge area, distinguishes observed conditions from assumptions, and explains what work is proposed. This avoids treating every crack as the same problem. It also helps homeowners compare written scopes rather than comparing slogans or broad claims.

Safe access is not optional

Ridge work is roof work at height. Roof tasks involve falls, access, weather, electricity and falling-object hazards, so Ellis plans access and site safety before work begins. Do not climb onto a tiled roof to press a ridge cap or scrape mortar. If a cap looks displaced after a storm, remain clear and contact Ellis Services Group.

Should ridge caps be repointed or re-bedded?

Bedding and pointing perform different jobs. Bedding is the mortar foundation beneath the ridge caps. Pointing is the exposed weathering finish over the bedding edges. A neat new pointing layer cannot correct ridge caps that have lost their supporting foundation. Ellis assesses both layers and the condition of the caps before choosing the repair.

When repointing addresses the problem

Repointing is appropriate when the caps remain secure, the bedding is sound and the exposed pointing has deteriorated. The work includes preparing the surface, removing loose material and applying a suitable finish to the affected ridge detail. Preparation and adhesion matter: applying new material over crumbling or contaminated surfaces leaves the underlying defect untreated.

When the bedding needs rebuilding

Movement, loose caps, broken bedding or loss of support call for a repair to the foundation. The affected caps are lifted, the damaged bedding is removed and the ridge is rebuilt with the caps aligned. Pointing then finishes the repaired detail. Damaged caps are assessed for replacement; the scope can cover a local section without assuming that every ridge on the roof needs the same treatment.

What Ellis checks around the ridge

We examine the junctions at hips, ridge ends and adjoining roof tiles, along with evidence of earlier patch repairs. A leak near a ridge still requires tracing: flashing, tile damage or another water path may also be involved. Before handover, the team checks the repaired caps, bedding and pointing finish and clears loose work debris.

From ground level, record obvious gaps, fallen fragments or changes after windy weather. Do not climb up to test whether a cap moves. Arrange an Ellis inspection through our ridge capping and tile repair service so the work matches the component that has actually failed.
